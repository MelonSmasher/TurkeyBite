# Lists and trust

Which lists may be `high` was measured, not assumed: removing one list at a time from the index, over a day of real DNS traffic and the Tranco top 100,000, showed which lists were the only reason an event carried a category. Broad ad, tracker and link-shortener lists (AdGuard DNS, Peter Lowe's, EasyPrivacy, frogeye's first-party trackers, WindowsSpyBlocker, Admiral and hagezi's URL shorteners) were `high`, and alone put `advertising` on product analytics such as `data.pendo.io` and `telemetry.canva.com` and `url-shorteners` on `box.com` and `forms.gle`; they are `medium` now, so a second independent list has to agree. The narrow vendor lists, which name one company's own domains, stay `high`, as does EasyList, which was the only source for 5 of the 20,000 most-queried hosts. `url-shorteners` is asserted by PeterDaveHello's curated list of about 1,400 active shorteners, which is `high`; hagezi's broader list of about 10,000, which also names link-in-bio pages and mail click trackers, corroborates nothing on its own. The AI list was `low` and never decided a category, so it was dropped; the AI category is carried by the local `turkeybite` list.

Which categories most traffic gets was measured the same way. The Université Toulouse Capitole (UT1) lists, which TurkeyBite already used for social networks and games, are a human-maintained categorisation rather than a block list, and eleven more of them are read at `high`: news (`press`), shopping, sports, webmail, streaming (`audio-video` and `radio`), file hosting, remote access tools (`remote-control`, which names TeamViewer, AnyDesk, ScreenConnect and the other tools a tech support scam installs), public encrypted DNS resolvers (`doh`, whose users TurkeyBite stops seeing), proxyware (`residential-proxies`, apps that sell a device's connection) and DDoS-for-hire services. Together they took the 10,000 most popular domains carrying any category from 19.4% to 23.1%, and those carrying a purpose from 14.0% to 17.4%, without one more threat category among them. Their mistakes are mild and are labels of what a site is for, such as `streaming` on sharethis.com and `remote-access` on webex.com, so the ignorelist is the place to correct them. UT1's VPN, gambling and warez lists are `medium`, corroborating the lists already there, and the categories it gets most wrong were left out: banking (lowes.com), jobs (bet365.com), chat (Microsoft's msedge.net), forums, blogs and dating. Its malware and phishing lists, which are nearly the same list, were left out too: they added no detection measured against OpenPhish's live phishing feed, and with one other list they put `malicious` on telegra.ph. CERT Polska's warning list was measured and left out because phishing.army already holds every name on it. abuse.ch's ThreatFox and URLhaus are `medium`: ThreatFox overlaps no list TurkeyBite reads, and took the share of its own malware names asserted from 4.6% to 8.7%, with the rest recorded as candidates, which is what the public resolvers below are asked about. Stalkerware comes from AssoEchap's indicators at `high`, which named one domain in the top 10,000, mobile-tracker-free.com, correctly. A deployment with its own `lists/host_files.json` reads only what that file names, so add these entries from `host_files.example.json` to it, or copy the example over it if you never changed it.
